From CA Rules to PSAK 118: Testing Listed Companies’ Readiness for 2027

PP 43/2025, the bank-specific CA requirement in POJK 18/2025, and PSAK 118: different scopes, one readiness agenda

By Hendrawanto, SE., Ak., CA., CLI., AseanCPA., CPA., CFI. · 23 October 2026 · Baca dalam Bahasa Indonesia

Heading into 2027, a listed company's readiness is not measured only by its ability to change the format of its statement of profit or loss. Preparer competence, traceability of figures, and consistent communication with investors are also tested. Three sets of rules meet here—PP 43/2025, POJK 18/2025, and PSAK 118—but each has a different scope.

An important boundary. In this article, "CA rules" refers mainly to the requirement under POJK 18/2025 that members of a bank's financial statement preparation team pass the Chartered Accountant (CA) certification exam. It does not mean every listed company must employ CA-qualified staff.

Three Rules, Three Functions

Teams need comparative data and an investor narrative.

PP 43/2025: competence must be evidenced, in more than one form

PP 43/2025 puts people and systems at the centre of financial reporting. The preparer is an employee of the reporting entity, and competence can be evidenced by a formal degree, an accounting expertise or professional certificate, or a registered accountant charter. Ministries, agencies, or authorities may set more specific competence types, considering business scale, industry, and the reporting entity's capacity. IAI stresses professional competence and preparer integrity.

A CA qualification is therefore strong evidence of professional capacity, but not the only route. Compliance should be assessed against the regulation, sector-specific rules, and the entity's own role and circumstances.

POJK 18/2025: an example of a more specific obligation

OJK confirms that the rule covers conventional commercial banks, sharia commercial banks, sharia business units, and branches of foreign banks. This is where an authority sets a more specific competence type: CA here is not merely an HR development preference but a requirement for members of a bank's preparation team. For public companies outside banking, the case for CA belongs to competence readiness and governance.

PSAK 118: The Same Profit, a More Structured Performance Story

PSAK 118 is based on IFRS 18 and mainly changes presentation, not the recognition or measurement of transactions. Income and expenses are grouped into operating, investing, financing, income tax, and discontinued operations categories. Entities present operating profit or loss, profit or loss before financing and income taxes, and profit or loss. Some classifications depend on the main business activity; banks and entities whose main business is investing or providing finance should not simply copy a non-financial company's example.

Another key change is management-defined performance measures (MPMs). When management communicates certain performance subtotals outside the financial statements and they meet the MPM definition, the entity must explain them in a single note, including a reconciliation to the most comparable SAK subtotal. This brings audited accounting figures together with the "adjusted" figures in investor presentations. See also PSAK 118: The New Profit or Loss Statement from 2027 and the PSAK 118 Statement of Profit or Loss format.

PT Sinar Laju Tbk (fictitious) is a non-financial manufacturing issuer. In its old format, interest and dividend income from investments was shown as "other income" above operating profit. This is not a new journal entry. PSAK 118's main impact lies in line-item mapping, presentation, and disclosure. If the underlying transaction requires a journal entry, it follows the relevant PSAK; PSAK 118 does not create it. What changes is the chart of accounts, the reporting map, and the data supporting the notes.

Where CA Adds Strategic Value

A CA qualification is relevant when an issuer needs consistent professional judgement where standards, systems, and capital markets meet. Professionals who understand the SAK framework can lead account mapping to the new categories, test subtotal consistency, assess whether an "adjusted" measure is an MPM, and prepare traceable reconciliations. The practical value is not the title alone: that competence must show in policy memos, change controls, team training, and the quality of audit committee review.

For listed banks, add a personnel compliance check against POJK 18/2025. For non-bank issuers, use CA as one option for building capacity, alongside sector experience, continuing education, and clear segregation of duties. An individual's title or certificate does not replace effective internal control.

Readiness Agenda to 2027

  1. 1Map regulatory scopeDetermine whether the entity is covered only by the general framework and PSAK 118, or also by bank-specific rules. Do not treat the bank checklist as a universal issuer checklist.
  2. 2Run a gap assessmentCompare the 2026 statement of profit or loss with the PSAK 118 structure; identify accounts whose classification needs judgement, especially where investing or financing is a main business activity.
  3. 3Prepare comparativesDesign account mapping, historical data, and change trails so 2026 comparatives can be restated. IFRS 18 applies retrospectively; final implementation follows the PSAK 118 text.
  4. 4Inventory "adjusted" measuresAlign investor presentations, press releases, and annual reports; identify measures that meet the MPM definition and design their reconciliations.
  5. 5Strengthen accountabilityReview the competence of preparers, reviewers, directors, and the audit committee. For banks, check the CA exam requirement; for non-banks, set a proportionate development plan.
  6. 6Test before go-liveRun a dry run for one period, review it with the external auditor and audit committee, then fix the consolidation system, note templates, and investor messaging.

Conclusion

Issuer readiness is not about renaming subtotals at the end of 2026. PP 43/2025 strengthens the competence foundation of reporting; POJK 18/2025 adds a CA requirement specific to bank preparers; and PSAK 118 changes the language of performance presentation from 2027. For non-bank issuers, CA can lift the quality of professional judgement, but it is not a general obligation arising from that POJK. Issuers that organise their people, data, policies, and communication now will be better placed to explain the same numbers with greater transparency.
