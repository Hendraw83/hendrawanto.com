PSAK 118 Statement of Profit or Loss: New Structure and Illustrative Format

Five categories, required subtotals, an example for a general non-financial entity, and adjustments for specified main business activities.

By Hendrawanto · 19 October 2026

PSAK 118 changes the structure of the statement of profit or loss through defined categories and subtotals. The change is significant, but it does not prescribe one rigid form that every company can copy.

On 28 May 2025, DSAK IAI approved PSAK 118 Presentation and Disclosure in Financial Statements. Based on IFRS 18, it replaces PSAK 201 and is effective from 1 January 2027. It primarily changes how financial performance is presented and explained rather than recognition or measurement.

Important limitation: the format below is designed for a general non-financial entity. Banks, finance companies, insurers and entities whose main business activity is investing in assets or providing financing to customers require a separate classification analysis.

Five categories in profit or loss

Income and expenses are classified into operating, investing, financing, income taxes and discontinued operations. Operating is the default category: an item that does not meet the requirements of the other four categories is classified as operating. It is therefore not limited to recurring items; volatile or unusual items may remain operating.

Operating

This category includes all income and expenses not classified in investing, financing, income taxes or discontinued operations. It provides a complete picture of an entity's operations during the period.

Investing

Investing generally includes returns from assets that generate returns individually and largely independently of other resources, as well as investments in associates and joint ventures.

Financing

Financing includes income and expenses from liabilities arising from financing transactions and interest or changes in interest rates on other liabilities.

Required subtotals

For a general non-financial entity, PSAK 118 results in three key totals or subtotals: operating profit or loss, profit or loss before financing and income taxes, and profit or loss for the year.

Gross profit, totals for investing or financing, profit before tax and profit from continuing operations may be presented when required by another standard or needed to provide a useful structured summary. Additional subtotals must be consistent, not misleading and no more prominent than required subtotals.

Not all interest income is investing

Automatically classifying all interest income as investing and all interest expense as financing is too simplistic. Classification changes when investing in assets or providing financing to customers is a main business activity.

For some entities, the profit before financing and income taxes subtotal may not be presented when the specified main-business-activity requirements make it irrelevant.

Expenses by nature, function or both

An entity presents operating expenses by nature, function or a mixture, selecting the approach that provides the most useful structured summary. Nature-based examples include raw materials, employee benefits, depreciation and advertising. Function-based examples include cost of sales, distribution, marketing and administration.

If one or more expense line items are presented by function, specified information about expenses by nature must still be disclosed in the notes. The format change can therefore require changes to the chart of accounts and supporting data.

Management-defined measures are not a sixth category

Adjusted EBITDA, core profit or normalised profit are not automatically required subtotals. If a subtotal used in public communications meets the definition of a management-defined performance measure, PSAK 118 requires disclosure in a single note, including calculation and reconciliation.

Transition to 2027

Calendar-year entities need the new structure for 2027 financial statements. Comparative 2026 information must be restated when PSAK 118 is first applied. Preparation should cover account mapping, assessment of main business activities, required and additional subtotals, disaggregation of “other” accounts, identification of management-defined measures, and changes to systems and controls.

Good implementation is more than relabelling rows. It requires understanding the source of each income and expense item and connecting primary statements to the notes.
