Revised ISA 240: The Auditor's Responsibilities Relating to Fraud

IAASB standard effective for periods beginning on or after 15 December 2026: scepticism, a fraud lens, responding to suspected fraud, and its position in Indonesia

By Hendrawanto, SE., Ak., CA., CLI., AseanCPA., CPA., CFI. · 13 October 2026 · Baca dalam Bahasa Indonesia

On 8 July 2025, the International Auditing and Assurance Standards Board (IAASB) issued ISA 240 (Revised), "The Auditor's Responsibilities Relating to Fraud in an Audit of Financial Statements". The international standard applies to audits of financial statements for periods beginning on or after 15 December 2026, so for calendar-year entities the 2027 financial statement audit will be the first affected in jurisdictions that adopt it.

Fraud is the risk that draws the most public attention when financial statements turn out to be wrong. The revision responds to criticism that auditors have been too passive when faced with signs of fraud. This article summarises the main changes, their position in Indonesia, and practical steps that boards of directors and audit committees can prepare.

Position in Indonesia

Public accountants in Indonesia apply the Standards on Auditing (SA) set by the Indonesian Institute of Certified Public Accountants (IAPI), including SA 240 on the auditor's responsibilities relating to fraud. The Standar Audit 2025 exposure draft issued by IAPI's Public Accountant Professional Standards Board, with a comment deadline of 10 October 2025, is based on the 2023–2024 edition of the IAASB Handbook, which predates ISA 240 (Revised). At the time of writing, the list of exposure drafts on IAPI's website does not yet include a revised SA 240.

This means the SA 240 currently in force remains the audit reference in Indonesia until IAPI decides otherwise. Even so, the direction of ISA 240 (Revised) is worth understanding now: Indonesia adopts ISAs periodically, and many of the practices strengthened by this revision can already be applied as good audit practice.

Five Key Changes

The IAASB highlights five main enhancements. They can be summarised as follows.

  1. Clearer auditor responsibilities. The auditor still does not make legal determinations of whether fraud has occurred, but is responsible for obtaining reasonable assurance that the financial statements are free from material misstatement due to fraud. The standard uses the term "fraud or suspected fraud", including allegations that come to the auditor's attention, and explicitly covers third-party fraud against the entity, for example by customers, suppliers or related parties.
  2. Reinforced professional scepticism. The auditor must remain alert to information indicating fraud risk factors, follow up inconsistent responses from management, those charged with governance or internal audit, and investigate further when conditions indicate that a document may not be authentic or that its terms have been modified without disclosure.
  3. Risk assessment through a "fraud lens". The engagement team discussion must address how and where the financial statements are susceptible to fraud, including culture and tone at the top. The auditor must understand the entity's whistleblower programme and how complaints are handled. The presumed risk of fraud in revenue recognition remains, and if the auditor concludes that the presumption does not apply, the reasons must be documented. The risk of management override of controls continues to be treated as a financial statement level risk.
  4. Responding to identified or suspected fraud. A new section sets out what the auditor does on identifying fraud or suspected fraud: understanding the matter, making inquiries of management and, where appropriate, those charged with governance, and evaluating the entity's investigation and remedial actions. The engagement partner determines whether additional risk assessment or further procedures are needed and assesses the effect on materiality, the reliability of management representations, other audit areas and prior periods.
  5. Communication and transparency. The auditor must communicate fraud or suspected fraud on a timely basis to management and those charged with governance, unless prohibited by law or regulation, and consider whether there is a duty or right to report it to an authority outside the entity. In audits applying the key audit matters requirements (ISA 701), the auditor determines key audit matters related to fraud and presents them under a subheading that shows their link to fraud.

Sharper Procedures

Beyond these five points, several familiar procedures are now set out in more detail. Journal entry testing includes inquiries of financial reporting staff, checking the completeness of the journal population, selecting period-end entries and considering whether to test entries throughout the period. Accounting estimates are reviewed for indicators of management bias, and significant unusual transactions are evaluated for whether their business rationale instead suggests fraudulent financial reporting or concealment of misappropriated assets.

Near the end of the audit, the auditor performs analytical procedures to detect previously unrecognised fraud risks and then re-evaluates whether the risk assessment and the evidence obtained are sufficient. Documentation is also expanded: team discussions, the basis for fraud risk assessments, conclusions on the revenue recognition presumption, results of procedures on management override, findings, and all fraud-related communications.

Link with ISA 570 (Revised 2024)

ISA 240 (Revised) takes effect at the same time as ISA 570 (Revised 2024) on going concern. The IAASB aligned the two because fraud and financial distress are often interrelated: pressure to meet targets or lender requirements can drive manipulation of financial statements. The IAASB also encourages jurisdictions to consider early adoption of both standards, together with narrow-scope amendments for listed entities, as a package.

What Companies Should Prepare

ISA 240 governs auditors, not management. However, because auditors will ask deeper questions and request more evidence, a well-prepared company will have a smoother audit. Areas worth reviewing:

  • A documented internal fraud risk assessment, including revenue, estimates and related party transactions.
  • A working whistleblowing channel, with records of complaints received, how they were reviewed and how they were followed up.
  • Controls over manual journal entries, especially period-end entries, entries by users with broad access, and entries without adequate supporting documents.
  • Clear internal investigation procedures for suspected fraud, including when to involve the audit committee and legal counsel.
  • Open communication with the auditor: suspected fraud, including fraud by third parties, is better disclosed early than discovered later by the auditor.

Audit committees can use audit planning to ask how the auditor assesses the company's fraud risks and which procedures will be performed in response.

Conclusion

ISA 240 (Revised) sharpens the auditor's role in dealing with fraud: more consistent scepticism, risk assessment through a fraud lens, clear steps when fraud is identified or suspected, and more open communication, including in the auditor's report. In Indonesia, the current SA 240 remains the reference until IAPI adopts the revision, but companies need not wait to strengthen their anti-fraud controls, complaint channels and journal entry documentation.
