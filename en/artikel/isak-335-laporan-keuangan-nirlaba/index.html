ISAK 335: Understanding Non-Profit Financial Statements

Linking donations, fund restrictions, surplus, and cash through a full case study, journal entries, and the June 2026 amendment towards PSAK 118

By Hendrawanto, SE., Ak., CA., CLI., AseanCPA., CPA., CFI. · 24 October 2026 · Baca dalam Bahasa Indonesia

Non-profit organisations need to explain what happened to the resources entrusted to them. Donors want to know how their support was used, boards need a picture of the ability to meet obligations, and supervisors need to judge whether funds were used for their intended purpose. This article links donations, fund restrictions, surplus, and cash through one case study whose figures can be traced from start to finish.

01 What ISAK 335 Does

A cash book records receipts and payments, but financial statements also need to explain assets, liabilities, and changes in the organisation's net worth. ISAK 335 Presentation of Financial Statements of Non-Profit Oriented Entities is an interpretation of the presentation requirements in the standard on presentation of financial statements. Its core is adjusting the descriptions of line items and the names of the statements to reflect the nature of a non-profit organisation. Recognition and measurement of transactions still follow the other standards in the framework the entity uses.

An education foundation may earn a surplus from training. That surplus does not change its social purpose. What matters is how activities are run, how the surplus is used, and what conditions attach to its funding. An organisation cannot be judged from a single surplus figure or bank balance.

PSAK 45, ISAK 35, and ISAK 335

DSAK IAI approved ISAK 35 and PPSAK 13 on 11 April 2019. ISAK 35 applies to annual periods beginning on or after 1 January 2020, together with the withdrawal of PSAK 45 on Financial Reporting of Non-Profit Entities through PPSAK 13. Under the SAK renumbering effective 1 January 2024, ISAK 35 became ISAK 335. This change of number is different from the substantive amendment approved in June 2026 (see section 09). IAI also states that the illustrative examples and basis for conclusions are not part of the interpretation.

02 Choosing the Right Reporting Framework

The reporting framework comes before the choice of format. This article uses SAK Indonesia for the 2026 illustration, assuming the entity has not early-applied PSAK 118. Organisations using SAK for Private Entities (SAK EP) follow that framework. SAK EP has been effective since 1 January 2025 and replaced SAK ETAP; eligibility depends on the entity's characteristics and the relevant authority's rules. See also SAK for Private Entities: A Transition Map from SAK ETAP.

Foundations (yayasan) and associations are typical settings. Organisations managing zakat, infaq, waqf, government funds, or other special transactions should also check the relevant standards and sector rules. Legal form or a "non-profit" label alone does not determine the full accounting treatment.

03 Reading Net Assets and Donor Restrictions

Net assets are total assets minus total liabilities. To help readers assess the use of resources, the illustration in this article distinguishes net assets without restrictions from resource providers and net assets with restrictions from resource providers. Restrictions that expire or are fulfilled result in a reclassification between classes of net assets.

SituationAnalysis needed
A donor gives support for the foundation's general activities.Presented without restrictions because the donor set no purpose or timing.
A donor specifies the support for student scholarships only.Use of funds is traced against the scholarship terms. Balances whose restrictions are not yet met are shown separately within net assets.
The board sets aside general funds for a planned renovation.An internal decision is identified separately. A board designation is not the same as a donor restriction.
An agreement requires refunds if certain conditions are not met.Review the substance of the agreement and the applicable recognition standard. The question may be about a liability or the timing of income, so it is not solved by labelling it "net assets with restrictions".

In the scholarship example below, the support already meets the criteria for recognition as income. The agreement only restricts its purpose and allows unused funds to be carried forward; there is no obligation to return the balance at the end of 2026. An agreement with different terms needs a fresh analysis.

Releasing a restriction does not increase total net assets. The reclassification reduces one class and increases the other by the same amount. Receiving a donation, paying programme expenses, and releasing a restriction are three different events that must be kept apart so income is not counted twice.

04 Five Components Read Together

The ISAK 335 illustrative examples include a statement of financial position, a statement of comprehensive income, a statement of changes in net assets, a statement of cash flows, and notes. Each answers a different question, and their figures lock into each other.

05 Case Study: Yayasan Cahaya Pendidikan

All names and figures below are fictitious. The statements simulate a full year ending 31 December 2026 with 2025 comparatives. This is a simplified teaching example, not an actual report or a full statement of compliance with SAK. 2025 is assumed to be the first year of activity with all opening balances at zero.

The foundation provides scholarships and training. At the start of 2026 it had cash of 120, net fixed assets of 100, and operating payables of 20. Its net assets were 200: 150 without restrictions and 50 restricted to scholarships. All figures are in Rp million; 400 means Rp400,000,000.

Scholarship donations in the example fund an operating programme, so their cash flows are operating. The 220 net asset reclassification is not a cash receipt or payment. E. Example notes to the financial statements

Basis of preparation. The example uses the accrual basis and historical cost; cash flows use the direct method; there is no other comprehensive income. Actual statements need the reporting framework, material policies, estimates, and other disclosures appropriate to the entity.

Scholarship donations. The opening balance of 50 plus receipts of 300 gives scholarship resources of 350. Disbursements of 220 met donor terms, so the same amount is released from restriction. The remaining 130 is for scholarships in future periods and is held entirely in cash, included in cash and bank of 400.

Fixed assets. Existing equipment cost 120 with accumulated depreciation of 20. Depreciation for 2026 of 20 brings the accumulated total to 40. New equipment of 60 was still being installed at year end and not yet ready for use, so in this example depreciation has not started. Closing carrying amount: 120 + 60 − 40 = 140.

Expenses by nature and function. The example allocates all depreciation to administration because the equipment in use supports that function. In practice, allocation follows asset use on a basis that can be explained.

08 Mistakes Boards Should Avoid

Treating the whole bank balance as free to spendOf cash of 400, 130 is for scholarships. Cash not tied to restrictions is 270; after payables of 30, 240 remains in this simple calculation. Cash planning must still allow for commitments and other operating needs.
Expensing the full cost of fixed assetsBuying equipment of 60 reduces cash and adds fixed assets. Depreciation is recorded separately when its conditions are met. Expensing everything distorts the surplus and assets.
Confusing project reports with entity statementsBudget-versus-actual reports for donors should be reconciled to the general ledger so that shared costs and inter-programme transactions can be traced.
Releasing restrictions just because cash went outCheck whether the spending meets donor purposes and conditions. An advance to a supplier does not necessarily mean the programme or conditions have been fulfilled.

Boards should keep a register of donation agreements, balances by programme, bank reconciliations, a fixed asset register, and beneficiary documents. Closing the books should link the opening balance, receipts, use, restriction releases, and closing balance of each fund. These reconciliations give a strong basis for oversight and audit.

09 The 2026 Amendment and Preparing for 2027

DSAK IAI approved the Amendment to ISAK 335 on 3 June 2026, as announced by IAI on 22 June 2026. The amendment updates references and illustrative examples to accommodate PSAK 118 for non-profit entities applying SAK Indonesia. The statement of financial performance format requires sub-classification into operating, investing, financing, income tax, and discontinued operations within the surplus or deficit section. For the indirect cash flow method, operating surplus is the starting point. The IAI announcement does not state the amendment's effective date; refer to the final text in SAK Online.

PSAK 118 was approved by DSAK IAI on 28 May 2025, replaces PSAK 201, and is effective 1 January 2027. Organisations using SAK Indonesia should review account mapping and comparatives before preparing statements under the new requirements. The 2026 example above does not assume early application. See PSAK 118: The New Profit or Loss Statement from 2027.

Mapping the same transactions. If all income and expenses in this case are operating, the operating surplus is 750 − 440 = 310. The investing and financing categories have no income or expenses, and income tax and discontinued operations are nil. The final surplus remains 310. The equipment purchase of 60 is an investing cash flow but does not automatically become an investing-category expense in the statement of financial performance.

With this simple data, starting from an operating surplus of 310 gives operating cash of 340 after adding depreciation of 20 and the increase in payables of 10. Organisations with investment income (for example interest on endowment deposits), financing costs, or special main activities need more detailed mapping. The identical figures here result from the assumptions, not proof that PSAK 118 never has an effect.

IAI's April 2026 public consultation material distinguished illustrations for SAK Indonesia and SAK for Private Entities. That material was a draft when issued; implementation of the amendment and its transition must follow the final text.

10 Judging the Statements Through Their Links

The foundation's statements can be traced from income of 750 and expenses of 440 to a surplus of 310. Opening net assets of 200 rise to 510. At the same time, closing cash of 400 is explained by opening cash of 120, operating cash of 340, and fixed asset purchases of 60. The remaining scholarship restriction of 130 appears in both net assets and cash.

These links let readers assess the organisation's ability to meet obligations while running its programmes. Boards need to keep the evidence behind the figures and the donor terms, so that the transparency of the statements can be tested again through the accounting records.
