Amending Tax Returns in Coretax: What Changed and What to Check

By Hendrawanto · October 18, 2026

Correcting a tax return is more than changing a number on a form. An amendment can affect the amount still payable, an overpayment claimed back, and a balance carried into a later period. Director General of Taxes Regulation PER-12/PJ/2026 changes several reporting provisions under Indonesia's Coretax administration. It was signed on 28 September 2026 and took effect on 1 October 2026. The practical questions are which return governs, which payments have already been counted, and what happens to any refund process in progress.

The central change: the latest amended return governs

Article 129A of PER-12/PJ/2026 provides that, where a taxpayer files an amended return, the exercise of tax rights and fulfilment of obligations are based on the latest amended return submitted. This is the starting point for interpreting the result. The exercise is not simply to enter a difference from a previous return; the amended return reflects the relevant overall position, with payments and refunds accounted for under the rules for that return type.

An explanation published by the Directorate General of Taxes (DJP) describes this as a replace scheme with a calculation rule. The description helps explain the system, but the binding details are in the regulation and its annex. Taxpayers should therefore review all relevant fields of the amended return rather than assume that entering only a net correction will suffice.

The regulation amends provisions covering, among others, monthly withholding returns for Income Tax Article 21/26, unified monthly income-tax returns, monthly VAT returns, and annual income-tax returns. Each has its own calculation provisions. For a monthly VAT amendment, Article 29B addresses earlier underpayment deposits and overpayment refunds already issued for that period. For annual income tax, Article 80A addresses Income Tax Article 29 payments and advance refunds already issued. A single, universal formula would overlook those distinctions.

The transition depends on more than the effective date

PER-12/PJ/2026 applies from 1 October 2026, but its transitional provision must also be read. A return or amendment filed before the effective date that has not yet been processed, or whose follow-up has not been completed, continues to be handled under PER-11/PJ/2025. By contrast, an amendment filed after the new regulation became effective to a return submitted earlier follows the new rules for tax periods from January 2025, parts of tax years ending from January 2025, or tax years from 2025. These distinctions are set out in Article II of PER-12/PJ/2026.

Two otherwise similar amendments may therefore be processed differently if they were filed on different dates or concern different tax periods. Before making a correction, identify the return type, the period or tax year, when the previous return was submitted, and when the amendment will be filed. At a transition boundary, rely on the actual transitional text and current DJP guidance, not a shorthand description of the “new scheme.”

Reconcile earlier payments and refunds

The practical purpose of the new calculations is to avoid reading an amendment as though payments or refunds already made did not exist. PER-12/PJ/2026 identifies the relevant components for each return type. Compare the final payable or refundable amount against payment records, relevant withholding or collection data, and any refund decision already issued.

This matters particularly when an original return showed an overpayment that changes on amendment. The numerical difference between two returns is not automatically a new tax debt. The outcome depends on payments made, refunds already issued, and the calculation rule for the relevant return. Conversely, a genuine underpayment shown after amendment may still need to be paid under the applicable provisions. Do not infer the tax position from a single field without reconciling the history.

Useful working papers include the original return and all its amendments, payment records, applicable withholding certificates, refund decisions, and an internal reconciliation. This is a suggested control checklist, not a universal statutory document requirement. If Coretax's displayed amounts differ from the evidence held by the taxpayer, resolve the discrepancy before submitting a further amendment or seek clarification through an official DJP channel.

A pending refund request can be affected

Amending a return that reports an overpayment may affect a refund request already made. Under Article 129A, certain requests are not pursued further if the return is amended before the specified procedural threshold is passed. For research into a refund of tax not legally due or an advance refund, the threshold concerns whether a decision has already been issued. For a refund through examination, the condition is that the examination has not yet begun. The provision also covers an advance-refund request made by separate letter.

This does not mean every refund request is automatically cancelled whenever a return is amended. The actual procedural stage must be checked. The regulation also requires notification for certain requests that will not be pursued further. A taxpayer awaiting a refund should establish that stage and the likely consequence before submitting another return. DJP's explanatory article can clarify the process, but a decision in a specific case must rest on its documents and the applicable rule.

There is another distinct consequence in Article 129B. If an annual return has already received approval to pay Income Tax Article 29 in instalments or to defer payment, that approval ceases to apply from the time the amended annual return is filed. A taxpayer relying on such an approval should assess this effect before filing, rather than assume the old payment schedule remains valid.

Compensation balances and later periods

For monthly VAT returns, Article 29C provides for an adjustment to an overpayment compensation balance when a return being amended carries an overpayment forward. The adjusted balance may be positive or negative and is taken into account in the first subsequent normal monthly VAT return not yet filed after the amended period. Article 29D also addresses adjustments arising from tax assessments, decisions, or appeal outcomes that affect the balance.

An amendment may therefore have effects beyond the return being corrected. Check whether the compensation carried forward remains consistent. Reconciling the source return, any relevant tax decision, and the next normal monthly return is more useful than judging an isolated positive or negative figure on screen. The regulation's annex and DJP explanatory material can help trace the filing examples.

Conclusion

PER-12/PJ/2026 changes how amended returns are treated in Coretax, but its application depends on the return type, tax period, and filing date. Before filing, ensure the latest amended return is complete, reconcile payments and refunds already made, confirm the stage of any refund request or instalment approval, and trace any effect on a compensation balance. Where an examination or special circumstance is involved, read the specific provisions and seek confirmation through official channels before acting.
