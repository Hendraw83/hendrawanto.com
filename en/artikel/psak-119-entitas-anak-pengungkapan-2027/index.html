PSAK 119: When Subsidiaries May Disclose Less Without Weakening the Accounting

Eligibility, the limits that still apply, the PSAK 119 amendments, and how groups can prepare before 1 January 2027

By Hendrawanto, SE., Ak., CA., CLI., ASEAN CPA, CPA., CFI. · 9 November 2026 · Baca dalam Bahasa Indonesia

Subsidiary financial statements often face a dilemma. The group needs figures prepared under aligned accounting policies, yet the readers of a subsidiary's statements rarely need notes as extensive as those required by investors in a listed company. As a result, subsidiary accounting teams often prepare dozens of pages of notes whose information is already in the parent's consolidated statements.

PSAK 119 Subsidiaries without Public Accountability: Disclosures offers a middle path: reduced disclosure requirements for eligible subsidiaries, while recognition, measurement, and presentation continue to follow other PSAKs (Indonesian Financial Accounting Standards). According to the Institute of Indonesia Chartered Accountants (IAI), its Financial Accounting Standards Board (DSAK IAI) approved PSAK 119 on 27 August 2025. The standard is based on IFRS 19, issued by the IASB in May 2024, and applies to reporting periods beginning on or after 1 January 2027, with earlier application permitted.

Who Can Apply It?

PSAK 119 is optional, not mandatory. According to the IAI, it may be chosen by a subsidiary that has no public accountability and whose parent prepares consolidated financial statements available for public use under SAK Indonesia, SAK Internasional, or IFRS Accounting Standards. All three conditions must be met at once.

Public accountability is assessed for the entity itself. Under IFRS 19 (paragraph 11), an entity has public accountability if its debt or equity instruments are traded in a public market or it is in the process of issuing them for trading in such a market, or if it holds assets in a fiduciary capacity for a broad group of outsiders as one of its primary businesses. Holding assets in a fiduciary capacity only incidentally to a primary business does not by itself create public accountability (paragraph 12). The final assessment still follows the PSAK 119 text and the sector regulations that apply in Indonesia.

Illustrative example. PT Induk publishes consolidated financial statements under SAK Indonesia. Its subsidiaries:

SubsidiaryMay it elect PSAK 119?
PT Anak Manufaktur: issues no securities and manages no third-party fundsLikely yes
PT Anak Finansial: has bonds listed on the stock exchangeNo
PT Anak Bank: takes deposits from the publicNo
PT Anak Asing: its foreign parent publishes IFRS consolidated statementsLikely yes

Being a subsidiary of a listed company is not enough if the subsidiary itself has public accountability. Conversely, the parent does not have to be listed; what matters is that its consolidated statements are publicly available and prepared under a recognised framework. This example does not determine eligibility for any particular company.

What Changes, and What Does Not?

PSAK 119 changes only the disclosures in the notes to the financial statements. An entity that elects it applies the PSAK 119 disclosure requirements in place of the disclosure requirements of other PSAKs. For recognition, measurement, and presentation, however, it continues to apply the other PSAKs.

PSAK 119 is therefore not permission to change the measurement basis of assets, remove liabilities, or calculate profit differently. A subsidiary's figures must still match what the consolidation package needs. Nor is PSAK 119 a reason to drop additional information that users need to understand the effect of particular transactions or conditions.

PSAK 119 also differs from SAK Entitas Privat (SAK EP), the private entity framework. SAK EP, which replaced SAK ETAP from 1 January 2025, is a separate framework that also simplifies some recognition and measurement principles. See the SAK EP transition map.

Why Do the 2025 Amendments Matter?

On 24 November 2025, DSAK IAI approved the Amendments to PSAK 119. They are based on the amendments to IFRS 19 issued by the IASB on 21 August 2025, and also take effect on 1 January 2027 with earlier application permitted. The May 2024 version of IFRS 19 covered IFRS requirements only up to February 2021; the amendments extend the reliefs to later changes. According to the IAI's summary, the amendments:

  1. change the provisions on application by an intermediate parent in its separate financial statements;
  2. remove disclosure objectives for supplier finance, lack of exchangeability, the Pillar Two model rules, classification and measurement of financial instruments, and non-current liabilities with covenants;
  3. reduce the disclosure requirements for supplier finance arrangements;
  4. remove material that is guidance rather than a disclosure requirement;
  5. replace the disclosure requirements for management-defined performance measures with a cross-reference to PSAK 118.

The implication: do not build a disclosure checklist from the original version of PSAK 119 alone. Use the standard together with its final amendments for the relevant period.

The link with PSAK 118 matters for groups that are also preparing the new profit or loss format and management-defined performance measure disclosures from 2027. See PSAK 118: The New Profit or Loss Statement from 2027.

Four Preparation Steps for Business Groups

1. Map potentially eligible entities. Document the control relationships, each entity's public accountability status at the end of the reporting period, and the framework and public availability of the parent's consolidated statements. This documentation also serves as evidence for the auditor.

2. Compare current notes with the final PSAK 119. Build a disclosure matrix by topic: retained, reduced, or no longer required. Do not assume every note can be removed.

3. Keep group figures and policies aligned. Reduced disclosures must not disrupt consolidation data. Detailed data no longer disclosed in the subsidiary's statements may still be needed in the reporting package to the parent.

4. Review with the auditor and audit committee before adoption. Consider the needs of report users, loan agreements that require particular disclosures, and sector regulations. From an audit perspective, the change in disclosure policy will be considered in assessing compliance with the reporting framework, including the wording of the compliance statement in the notes.

Conclusion

PSAK 119 is a more proportionate reporting option, not "lighter accounting" for every subsidiary. Its benefits arise when a group assesses eligibility correctly, uses the standard with its final amendments, and shortens the notes without losing important information. Ahead of 2027, the main task is not new journal entries but deciding who is eligible and which disclosures are still needed.
