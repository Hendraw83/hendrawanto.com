New PSAK 408: Understanding Sharia Insurance Accounting Ahead of 2029

Contribution allocation, risk measurement models, and what to prepare before implementation

By Hendrawanto, SE., Ak., CA., CLI., ASEAN CPA, CPA., CFI. · 19 November 2026 · Baca dalam Bahasa Indonesia

The Institute of Indonesia Chartered Accountants (IAI) announced the approval of PSAK 408: Sharia Insurance Accounting on 12 November 2026. According to the announcement, IAI's Sharia Accounting Standards Board (DSAS) approved the standard on 22 July 2026. The new standard replaces PSAK 408 on Accounting for Sharia Insurance Transactions issued in 2016 and is effective for financial years beginning on or after 1 January 2029.

These three dates need to be kept apart: the announcement date, the approval date, and the effective date. An implementation plan built on the wrong date, for example the date proposed in the exposure draft, produces a project timeline that is wrong from the start. PSAK 408 belongs to the 4xx series for sharia standards; how that numbering works is explained in the PSAK and ISAK Number Conversion Table.

Why Does This Change Matter?

IAI describes significant changes to sharia insurance accounting, particularly in the initial recognition of contributions, the measurement of insurance risk, and the recognition of fees (ujrah) and expenses. That focus shows the update reaches into how sharia insurance contracts are translated into financial statement figures. Readers need to look at the rights and obligations arising from the contract, not just the amount of contribution cash received.

According to IAI's announcement, the new requirements aim to produce more relevant financial information in depicting the rights, obligations, and performance arising from sharia insurance contracts, and are expected to improve the transparency and comparability of financial statements, giving users clearer information about financial position, performance, and risk.

The standard covers the recognition, measurement, presentation, and disclosure of contracts used in sharia insurance. IAI's final announcement names tabarru', tanahud, and wakalah as examples of contract types whose treatment is expected to become more consistent. In its exposure draft announcement, IAI explained that tabarru' and tanahud are used among participants to share insurance risk (risk sharing), while wakalah is used to manage that risk. This helps explain why the whole contribution cannot simply be read as one uniform economic flow. The detailed accounting for each contract type must still be read from the final text of the standard, not inferred from the announcement.

Participant Contributions Are Allocated from the Start

One of the key points stated by IAI is the allocation of contributions at initial recognition into insurance risk, fee (ujrah), and investment components. Each has a different economic function. Accounting processes therefore need to be able to identify the relevant components from the contract terms and transaction data.

If a system stores only total contributions without tagging their components, an entity may face look-back work when preparing reports under the new standard. This is an operational implication drawn from the direction of the requirements, not a specific system obligation stated in IAI's announcement.

In preparing, an entity can review contract documents, the definition of contribution for each product, and the data flows that generate accounting entries, so that the risk, fee, and investment portions can be traced back to source documents. Aligning terminology across product development, operations, actuarial, and accounting also reduces differences in interpretation. These steps are general readiness suggestions; classification and measurement decisions still require the final text of PSAK 408 and the actual facts of each contract.

Two Models for Measuring Insurance Risk

PSAK 408 introduces a general measurement model for contracts with a coverage period of more than 12 months and a simplified measurement model for contracts with a coverage period of up to 12 months. The threshold concerns insurance risk, so the analysis should not stop at product labels or legacy accounting habits. The coverage period needs to be read from the relevant contract terms.

The simplified model does not mean entries can be made without analysis. It is still a measurement model within the standard, with a scope and application requirements that must be read from the final text. Likewise, use of the general model cannot be decided solely from the contract length shown in marketing materials if the contract documents and contract boundary indicate otherwise. IAI also refers to requirements on aggregation and contract boundaries, so mapping contracts and contract groups is an important part of preparation. This article deliberately does not present measurement formulas because the details are not set out in the announcements on which it is based.

Fees, Expenses, and Derecognition

Besides risk measurement, IAI highlights methods for recognising fees (ujrah) and expenses and the derecognition of sharia insurance contracts. These can be seen as a single reporting flow: data at contract inception determines which components are tracked, events during the coverage period affect subsequent recognition, and the end of a contract requires a clear basis for derecognition. This flow is a way of organising implementation work, not a statement that every contract follows the same recognition pattern.

For preparers, a readiness check can start with a few questions:

  1. Are contract documents available, up to date, and linkable to each policy or membership?
  2. Can contributions be split into insurance risk, fee (ujrah), and investment components?
  3. Can coverage periods and contract boundaries be determined consistently to select the measurement model?
  4. Are the events that end a contract documented so that derecognition can be traced?

The answers help identify data gaps before detailed calculations are made. Accounting policies and system changes are best finalised after the final PSAK 408 has been read in full through SAK Online, as IAI directs.

Do Not Use the Draft Date as the Final Date

DSAS IAI approved the Exposure Draft (ED) of PSAK 408 on 22 December 2025, and IAI announced it on 20 January 2026 with a proposed effective date of 1 January 2028 and a public comment deadline of 13 March 2026. The later approval announcement sets 1 January 2029. In other words, the draft date changed in the final decision. The ED remains useful for understanding how the discussion evolved, but it does not replace the approved text. Older articles or presentations that still cite the proposed date should be updated.

The approval announcement does not mention early application. If an entity is considering it, the transition and early application requirements must be checked directly in the final text. An overview of other standards coming into effect is available in Implementing the Latest SAK for Business.

Conclusion

The approval of PSAK 408 sets a new direction for accounting for sharia insurance contracts: contributions are allocated at initial recognition, insurance risk is measured using the model that fits the coverage period, and the recognition of fees, expenses, and contract derecognition is addressed in a more structured way. The most useful preparatory work is mapping the contracts and data flows that will support implementation. For detailed accounting decisions, use the final text of PSAK 408 on SAK Online and make sure project timelines refer to the effective date of 1 January 2029, not the date proposed in the exposure draft.
