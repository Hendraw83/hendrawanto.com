SIMMKAP and Audit Firm Quality Management: The App Helps, Responsibility Stays with the Firm

The role of the application in mapping quality risks, designing responses, and assessing how the system operates

By Hendrawanto, SE., Ak., CA., CLI., ASEAN CPA, CPA., CFI. · 24 November 2026 · Baca dalam Bahasa Indonesia

In an official release dated 5 November 2026, the Indonesian Institute of Certified Public Accountants (IAPI) encouraged public accounting firms (KAP) to use the Public Accounting Firm Quality Management System Application (SIMMKAP). The application was developed by IAPI together with the Directorate of Financial Profession Development and Supervision of the Ministry of Finance and the Financial Services Authority (OJK) as a tool to support the implementation and documentation of quality management systems more systematically. The same release draws an important boundary: SIMMKAP is a supporting tool, while responsibility for implementing and evaluating the quality management system remains with each firm.

This message matters for firm leaders and personnel assessing their quality readiness. Neatly stored documents make tracing easier, but the quality of a system cannot be judged from the number of files or the status of an application account alone. What needs to be examined is whether quality objectives are understood, real risks have been identified, responses are carried out, and deficiencies are followed up. This article explains how to use the application as an aid without replacing the firm’s professional judgment.

The Standard Places Responsibility on the Firm

According to IAPI’s Quality Management Standards page, SMM 1 deals with a firm’s responsibility to design, implement, and operate a system of quality management for audits or reviews of financial statements, other assurance engagements, or related services engagements. SMM 1 applies to all firms that perform any of these engagements. IAPI states that the Quality Management Standards have been in effect since 31 December 2025.

The word “design” requires a firm to tailor the system to its own circumstances. Engagement patterns, staffing, reliance on service providers, and supervision practices differ between firms, so a uniform list of policies does not necessarily address the risks at every firm. In its explanation of the Quality Management Toolkit, IAPI states that firms must tailor quality risks and responses to their nature and circumstances, and reminds them that the toolkit does not replace the obligation to read SMM 1.

“Implement” means putting the design into work processes: policies on engagement acceptance, consultation, supervision, and handling of findings must be usable by personnel at the moment decisions are made. “Operate” requires the system to run continuously, not only to be filled in ahead of an inspection. This distinction is a practical way of reading the three responsibilities in SMM 1, not a complete list of the standard’s requirements.

What Does SIMMKAP Help With?

According to the IAPI release, SIMMKAP is designed to help firms document the assignment of responsibilities, the identification and assessment of quality risks, the design of responses to those risks, and policies and procedures relevant to each firm’s circumstances. Consistently organised information makes it easier for those responsible to trace the link between a risk and the response designed for it.

For example, when a firm identifies the risk of late review of work, the system records can show who is responsible, which process was agreed, and what evidence will later show that the review took place. This example is illustrative. Decisions on the relevance of a risk, the design of a response, and the sufficiency of evidence are still made on the basis of facts at the firm, not taken automatically from application fields or templates.

Structured records also support continuity of knowledge when assignments change: policies, reasons for changes, and the results of follow-up are easier to understand for personnel taking over. That benefit only materialises when information is kept current and used in real decisions. An active account without up-to-date content does not show that quality risks are under control.

Do Not Equate Registration with Compliance

IAPI reported that as of 4 November 2026, 134 firms were recorded in the SIMMKAP registration follow-up process; 99 firms had obtained registration approval and 35 firms were still in process. IAPI itself reads these figures as participation in the early stage of using the application. They are not the result of a quality assessment of each firm, so registration status must not be equated with a conclusion that a firm’s quality management system meets the requirements.

This separation matters in internal communication. If leadership receives a report that “the system is done” because the account has been approved, the next questions should go to substance: do the mapped quality risks reflect practice? Does each response have a clear owner? Do personnel understand when and how procedures are performed? Do identified weaknesses lead to change? These questions shift attention from administrative milestones to real quality work. IAPI likewise stresses that implementing SMM is not only about complete documentation but also requires leadership commitment and the involvement of personnel in applying quality policies and procedures consistently.

Connect Documents with Evidence of Performance

A useful first step is to trace a few key processes from start to finish:

  1. Take one quality objective and assign its owner.
  2. Look at the quality risks that could hinder it and the reasons for their assessment.
  3. Check the chosen responses: policies, procedures, and who performs them.
  4. Match them with evidence of performance in actual engagements or processes.
  5. Compare with evaluation records; if the chain is broken, fix the rationale, the owner, or the procedure.

The example in the figure shows the difference. A written policy may require consultation on difficult technical matters. Evidence of performance consists of records of the question, who was consulted, the views given, and how the conclusion affected the work. If documents only say “consultation was undertaken” without being traceable to a decision, the firm should consider whether its documentation is sufficient for quality management purposes.

Reviews should also leave room for feedback from personnel. A procedure that looks complete on screen may be too complex, poorly understood, or out of step with the workflow. Input from those who perform the work helps leadership distinguish training gaps from design gaps, and then becomes the basis for revising responses and updating documents, so that the application reflects a living system.

Use Support Resources for Their Intended Purpose

The IAPI release mentions three kinds of support. First, a helpdesk at IAPI’s office in East Jakarta for registration, account activation, and technical issues with the application, with plans to extend helpdesk and training services through Regional Coordinators. Second, a two-stage workshop: an online session on using the application on 23 November 2026 and an in-person session on practical SMM implementation through SIMMKAP on 25–26 November 2026. Participants are expected to be their firm’s SIMMKAP PIC, to have registered an account, and to bring their firm’s SMM design; the full series carries 20 structured audit CPD credits (SKP). Third, IAPI has made available the Quality Management Toolkit and Illustrative Quality Risk Matrix as illustrative documentation.

Technical support resolves registration or operating issues with the application; questions about professional obligations are still answered by reference to SMM 1, not interface instructions. Firms can therefore use resources in sequence: read the standard to understand responsibilities, analyse the firm’s circumstances to design responses, use the toolkit as illustration, then document decisions and their performance in a suitable tool. This sequence keeps the tool in service of quality control needs. A related discussion of technology-based tools within a quality framework is in IAASB Guide on Quality Management for the Use of AI Tools in Audit, while the narrow-scope amendments to international quality and auditing standards relating to publicly traded entities are covered in Publicly Traded Entity Definition in Auditing Standards.

Conclusion

SIMMKAP can clarify the links between responsibilities, quality risks, responses, and documentation. However, the success of a quality management system depends on a design that fits the firm’s circumstances, implementation in the work, and honest evaluation of deficiencies. IAPI stresses that the application is a supporting tool and that responsibility for quality remains with the firm. A more meaningful measure of success is whether the system actually helps personnel produce work that is consistent and traceable, not merely whether an account has been approved.
