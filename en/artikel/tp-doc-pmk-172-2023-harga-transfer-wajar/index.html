TP Documentation Is More Than Compliance: How to Prove Related-Party Prices Are Arm's Length

Thresholds, contents, and deadlines for transfer pricing documentation under PMK 172/2023, and how to build evidence that holds up

By Hendrawanto, SE., Ak., CA., CLI., ASEAN CPA, CPA., CFI. · 5 November 2026 · Baca dalam Bahasa Indonesia

Companies within a group often sell goods to one another, provide services, lend money, or use group brands and technology. Those transactions are legitimate. What must be explainable is the price: would the company agree to the same terms with a party that has no special relationship with it?

That is the role of transfer pricing documentation, or TP Doc. Minister of Finance Regulation (PMK) 172 of 2023 calls it Dokumen Penentuan Harga Transfer. It supports the application of the arm's length principle (Prinsip Kewajaran dan Kelaziman Usaha, PKKU) and is more than an attachment to the tax return. PMK 172/2023 replaced the earlier documentation rules in PMK 213/2016 and brought the mutual agreement procedure (MAP) and advance pricing agreement (APA) rules into one regulation.

Three Layers of Documentation

Article 16(2) of PMK 172/2023 divides transfer pricing documentation into three documents with different purposes.

Not every company must prepare all three. The master and local files have their own criteria, while the country-by-country report (CbCR) applies only to groups that meet additional criteria. Documentation is prepared in Indonesian unless the taxpayer has permission to keep its books in a foreign language.

Who Must Prepare a Master File and a Local File?

There are two layers of conditions. First, the taxpayer enters into related-party transactions in the tax year concerned. Second, the taxpayer meets any one of the following criteria, measured in the previous tax year (Article 16(3)):

Criterion (previous tax year)Threshold
Gross turnoverMore than IDR 50 billion
Related-party transactions in tangible goodsMore than IDR 20 billion
Each type of related-party transaction in services, interest, use of intangibles, or other related-party transactionsMore than IDR 5 billion
Transactions with a related party in a country or jurisdiction whose income tax rate is lower than the Indonesian corporate rate under Article 17 of the Income Tax LawNo monetary threshold

The words "more than" matter: an amount exactly equal to a threshold does not meet that monetary criterion, although another criterion may still apply. Thresholds are also not added across transaction types; services are tested against IDR 5 billion and tangible goods against IDR 20 billion. If the previous tax year was shorter than 12 months, the amounts are annualised first (Article 16(6)).

For the CbCR, watch the reference year. The IDR 11 trillion threshold is measured in the tax year before the reported year, so 2025 consolidated turnover decides the 2026 CbCR obligation. A CbCR analysis must also consider the group structure and the exchange-of-information mechanism between countries, not only the turnover of the Indonesian entity.

Falling below the TP Doc thresholds does not exempt a company from the arm's length principle. Article 3 of PMK 172/2023 requires every related-party transaction to be arm's length. The thresholds only remove the obligation to keep the master and local file format, not the substantive obligation to price transactions properly. The DJP can still test those transactions.

When Must TP Documentation Be Ready and Submitted?

The master file and local file must be available no later than four months after the tax year-end, together with a statement on when they became available (Article 18(1)). The CbCR is due within 12 months after the tax year-end (Article 18(2)). The master and local file summaries are attached to the annual corporate income tax return for the same tax year; proof of CbCR filing is attached to the return for the following tax year (Article 19).

The full documents are not attached to the return, but they must be ready on request. For compliance monitoring or a tax audit, the taxpayer must hand them over within one month of the request date (Article 34(2)).

Preparing TP documentation only after a request letter arrives is risky. Comparable data may be hard to obtain, the reasons for choosing a method may never have been recorded, and the description of each party's functions may differ from the contracts and from actual conduct. Documents that are late or not submitted also weaken the taxpayer's position if prices are adjusted. The DJP's CbCR page also refers to administrative sanctions under the General Tax Provisions Law (UU KUP) for unmet reporting obligations.

Example: Intra-Group Management Services

PT Aruna pays IDR 8 billion to an affiliate for management services during 2026. Assume its related-party service payments in 2025 also exceeded IDR 5 billion. Absent any other exception, the monetary criterion for a 2026 master and local file is met. Had the 2025 service payments been exactly IDR 5 billion, with no other criterion met, no 2026 TP Doc format obligation would arise. The 2026 service fee would still have to be arm's length.

Good documentation does not stop at "the cost was IDR 8 billion". For service transactions, Article 13(1) of PMK 172/2023 requires preliminary steps before the price itself is tested:

Supporting evidence includes contracts, work reports, correspondence, minutes, and the cost allocation basis. The analysis should also set out each party's functions, assets, and risks, the reasons for the chosen method, and the comparison with relevant comparables. If the evidence is only invoices and a generic contract, the existence of a cost does not prove that the service delivered a benefit or that its price is arm's length.

A sound analysis connects business events, accounting figures, the transfer pricing method, and tax reporting. This example is illustrative; a real tax conclusion depends on the facts of the transaction and a complete analysis.

Five Practical Steps to Prepare TP Documentation

First, map every special relationship and related-party transaction. Reconcile the related-party list in the financial statements with the general ledger, contracts, payments, and tax forms, and trace every difference between sources. Related parties under PSAK 224 are not always identical to parties with a special relationship under tax rules, such as ownership of at least 25% under PMK 172/2023.

Second, test the documentation obligation each year. Use the previous year's gross turnover and related-party transaction values to decide whether the master and local file thresholds are met. Test the CbCR obligation separately at group level, and do not forget the notification.

Third, understand the transaction before choosing a method. The characterisation of functions, assets, and risks must match actual conduct. PMK 172/2023 offers several methods, including the comparable uncontrolled price (CUP), resale price, cost plus, profit split, and transactional net margin (TNMM) methods. A method that looks right on paper can fail if its business assumptions are wrong.

Fourth, build evidence as you go. Article 17(1) requires the master and local files to be prepared from the data and information available when the related-party transactions take place. Pricing policies and supporting documents should therefore be maintained throughout the year, not reconstructed afterwards.

Fifth, check consistency before filing the return. Make sure the TP Doc, the summary, the financial statements, and the return describe the same transactions. For businesses with activities of different character, the local file is presented by segment (Article 30). This consistency also makes it easier to respond to questions from the tax office; see also amending tax returns in Coretax.

Conclusion

Strong TP documentation is not a thick file that simply declares prices to be arm's length. It is a chain of evidence explaining why a related-party transaction makes business sense and how its price was tested. Start by mapping the transactions and obligations, then build the documentation while the information is still available. For business groups, the 2025 notification and CbCR deadline of 31 December 2026 is the nearest checkpoint. When an audit comes, the quality of the argument will matter more than the page count.
