KPK Responds to Proposal to Remove the State Loss Element from the Definition of Corruption

The Corruption Eradication Commission (KPK) responded to the National Economic Council (DEN) proposal to change the definition of corruption, including the state financial loss element. According to KPK, state financial loss is expressly regulated as one of 30 types of corruption offenses in the Anti-Corruption Law (UU Tipikor), has been carried into Articles 603 and 604 of the National Criminal Code (KUHP), and has repeatedly been reviewed by the Constitutional Court. KPK considers it more urgent to criminalize UNCAC offenses not yet covered, such as foreign bribery, private-sector bribery, trading in influence, and illicit enrichment.

DEN: Proposed Corruption Definition Keeps Accountability for State Loss

DEN Chair Luhut Binsar Pandjaitan stated that the recommended amendments to the Anti-Corruption Law do not remove the state financial loss element. Articles 2 and 3 of the Anti-Corruption Law and Articles 603 and 604 of the Criminal Code remain; what is refined is how the elements are proven. DEN recommends four steps: refining those articles, adding protection for good-faith decisions (a business judgment rule), adopting UNCAC offenses not yet regulated, and drafting an omnibus law on state finances.

Police Say BPKP and Public Accountants Can Audit State Financial Loss

In a judicial review hearing at the Constitutional Court on the Elucidation of Article 603 of the Criminal Code (KUHP) (case 206/PUU-XXIV/2026), the Indonesian National Police (Polri) stated that BPKP, APIP, and public accountants, within their respective authority, are competent to examine and calculate state financial loss. Their findings may serve as investigative material or as evidence assessed by the judges. Polri emphasized that the final determination of the amount of state loss remains under the authority of BPK.

The Constitutional Court’s New Interpretation of State Financial Loss in the Government Administration Law

In Decision No. 66/PUU-XXIV/2026, the Constitutional Court partly granted a judicial review of Law 30/2014 on Government Administration. The phrase “state loss” (kerugian negara) in Article 20(5) and (6) was declared conditionally unconstitutional unless read as “state financial loss” (kerugian keuangan negara). Reimbursement falls on the government agency when an administrative error occurs without abuse of authority, and on the government official when abuse of authority is involved.

Behind the Constitutional Court Ruling on ‘State Loss’: DPR Expert Body Calls for Technical Rule Adjustments

In Decision No. 28/PUU-XXIV/2026, the Constitutional Court rejected all petitions for judicial review of the National Criminal Code, so Articles 603 and 604 remain in force. In its legal reasoning, the Court stated that the phrase “causing loss to state finances” in Article 603 must refer to the audit findings of the Audit Board (BPK) under Article 23E(1) of the 1945 Constitution. The DPR Expert Body considers that the ruling does not change any norm, so follow-up only requires technical regulatory adjustments.

Kaur District Prosecutor's Office Names Former Kaur KPU Secretary as Suspect in Alleged Corruption of 2022 KPU Grant Funds

The Kaur District Prosecutor's Office named the former Secretary of the Kaur Regency KPU, who in 2022 served as both budget user proxy (KPA) and commitment officer (PPK), as a suspect in the alleged corruption of election activity grant funds worth approximately Rp1.07 billion.
