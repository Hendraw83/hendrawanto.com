BPK's Calculation in the Hajj Quota Corruption Case Laid Out in Court

At a hearing in the Jakarta Corruption Court on 27 October 2026, BPK auditor Muhammad Ramadhani, called by KPK prosecutors as an expert, explained the calculation of the Rp622.09 billion state financial loss in the 2023–2024 additional hajj quota case. The figure consists of the difference between special hajj operators' (PIHK) receipts and spending for 8,151 special pilgrims in 2024 who were not entitled to depart (±Rp438.2 billion), the sale of 2024 special hajj officer quota slots (±Rp40.0 billion), and acceleration fees in 2023 and 2024 (±Rp143.9 billion). Luhut confirms the state loss article will not be removed from the Anti-Corruption Law

National Economic Council (DEN) chairman Luhut Binsar Pandjaitan stated that DEN has made no recommendation to remove the state loss article from the Anti-Corruption Law. DEN proposes strengthening Articles 2 and 3 of the Anti-Corruption Law and Articles 603 and 604 of the Criminal Code through a criminal intent (mens rea) element in line with international standards and Constitutional Court Decision 28/PUU-XXIV/2026, a business judgment rule, new offences such as bribery of foreign public officials, and an omnibus law on state finance to clarify the boundary of state-owned enterprise assets. The proposals were discussed on 27 October 2026 with the Coordinating Ministry for Law, Human Rights, Immigration and Corrections, the Ministry of Law, the Presidential Staff Office, the Attorney General's Office, the National Police and the KPK; the KPK recommended a standard method for calculating state losses by authorised institutions. The government will further refine the recommendations.

Yusril Calls for a Clearer Definition of State Finances, Flags Risk of SOE Business Losses Becoming Corruption Cases

Coordinating Minister for Law, Human Rights, Immigration and Corrections Yusril Ihza Mahendra said the definition of state finances needs to be clarified to provide legal certainty, particularly in determining whether losses at state-owned enterprises (SOEs) can be classified as corruption. He questioned the status of separated state assets used as SOE capital, the assets of SOE subsidiaries, and bad loans at state-owned banks whose funds come from public deposits. In his view, losses arising from business risk or technical banking issues need clear limits so they are not automatically brought under corruption articles. The review aims to align Law 31/1999 as amended by Law 20/2001 with the UN Convention against Corruption (UNCAC).

Banten RayaState Loss23 October 2026

State Loss Is Not a Guessing Figure (Kerugian Negara Bukan Angka Tebakan)

An opinion piece by Hendrawanto in Banten Raya's Opinion section, responding to the debate over the National Economic Council (DEN) proposal and its clarification that state financial loss remains an element of the Anti-Corruption Law. The author argues that the main issue is the reliability of loss figures, which often shape public perception before any verdict. The piece outlines the existing safeguards: the definition of state loss in the State Treasury Law, Constitutional Court Decision 25/PUU-XIV/2016 (actual loss, not potential loss), and Constitutional Court Decision 66/PUU-XXIV/2026. It also identifies three recurring errors in loss calculations and recommends that auditors separate administrative noncompliance, actual loss, potential loss, and nonfinancial impact, and disclose the methods they use.

The Constitutional Court’s New Interpretation of State Financial Loss in the Government Administration Law

In Decision No. 66/PUU-XXIV/2026, the Constitutional Court partly granted a judicial review of Law 30/2014 on Government Administration. The phrase “state loss” (kerugian negara) in Article 20(5) and (6) was declared conditionally unconstitutional unless read as “state financial loss” (kerugian keuangan negara).
